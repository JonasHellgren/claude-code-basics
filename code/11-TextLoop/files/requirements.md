# Text Requirements

## Structure

- Create at least one paragraph for each pole in `poles.md`.
- Keep the paragraphs in the same order as the poles.
- When a pole feels to short or dry, add relevant text and statements. See Pole extension examples
- Do not omit any pole.
- Do not add new topics that are not supported by the poles.
- Do not use headings inside the final text.
- If motivated, change the order of poles/paragraphs. This can be motivated by flow. For example, 
it is often natural to start with the objective.

## Length

- Each paragraph should contain between 25 and 150 words.
- The complete text must contain no more than 600 words. Corresponding to one a4 page.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition.

## Language

- Write in simple English.
- No evident AI generated text, see Natural Writing Style
- Prefer short sentences.
- Avoid unexplained technical terms.
- Use correct grammar, spelling, and punctuation.

## Natural Writing Style
Avoid text patterns commonly associated with AI-generated writing.
Do not use em dashes (—). Use commas, periods, parentheses, or colons instead.
Avoid excessive headings, bullet lists, and numbered lists.
Avoid repetitive sentence structures.
Avoid unnecessary summaries or conclusions.
Do not repeat the same point using different words.
Avoid generic phrases such as “It is important to note,” “In conclusion,” and “Overall.”
Avoid exaggerated or promotional words such as “crucial,” “powerful,” “seamless,” “robust,” and “revolutionary.”
Do not use artificial contrasts such as “It is not only X, but also Y” unless necessary.
Avoid repeatedly starting sentences with linking words such as “Furthermore,” “Moreover,” and “Additionally.”
Prefer direct, natural sentences with varied lengths.
Write like a knowledgeable person, not like a chatbot.
The reviewer must mark the requirement as FAIL if any em dash or obvious AI-style pattern remains.

## Pole extension examples
Pole: The reviewer checks the text.
Expanded: The reviewer checks the text for clarity, structure, grammar, and compliance with the requirements. It identifies weaknesses and gives specific feedback to the modifier.

Pole: The modifier updates the draft.
Expanded: The modifier updates the draft based on the reviewer’s feedback. It improves weak sections while preserving parts that already meet the requirements.

Pole: The loop continues until the text is good enough.
Expanded: The loop continues until the reviewer finds no important problems and the text satisfies all defined requirements. Minor stylistic issues should not prevent completion.

Pole: Agent memory changes over time.
Expanded: Agent memory changes over time as new experiences are added, useful information is reinforced, and outdated details are removed. This allows the agent to adapt its behavior gradually.

Pole: Animations illustrate the process.
Expanded: Animations illustrate how information enters the memory, is evaluated, and is either retained, updated, or forgotten. This makes the evolution of the agent’s memory easier to understand.

Pole: The agent learns from mistakes.
Expanded: The agent learns from mistakes by comparing its output with feedback and expected results. It can then adjust its future decisions to avoid repeating the same errors.

## Abreviations to not change
Following abreviations shall not be changed or removed: REF, FIG, TAB.

## Quality threshold

The reviewer gives the text a score from 1 to 10.

The text is approved when:

- every mandatory requirement is satisfied
- there are no major language errors
- the score is at least 9 out of 10

The process must stop after a maximum of 5 iterations.
