Artificial intelligence agents are transforming how work gets done. They automate complex tasks, support decision-making, and take over execution at scale. This shift affects many kinds of work, not just a few specialized fields. Understanding what these systems can do is essential for anyone working with modern technology.

The term "agent" covers many different approaches, which makes the concept seem unclear. Broadly speaking, an AI agent is a system that perceives its environment and takes actions toward defined goals. This text explores two major types: those built on large language models and those trained through reinforcement learning.

Large language model agents receive instructions in plain English, often written in markdown files. These systems excel at tasks such as creating, editing, and summarizing text. Beyond writing, they can reason through problems and generate code. A programmer can describe what they need, and the agent builds it. This flexibility makes them useful for many kinds of knowledge work.

Reinforcement learning agents REF operate differently. Written in traditional programming languages, they learn by trial and error inside structured environments. These agents solve problems in robotics and control systems, where precise action matters. Rewards and penalties guide their progress, helping them discover better strategies through repeated exploration.

The two types differ in important ways. Reinforcement learning agents have been around much longer in AI research, and they require deep mathematical knowledge to design and train effectively. Large language model agents are newer but easier to use without specialized AI expertise, since they work with natural language instead of mathematical formulas.

Neither type is inherently better. Each excels in different contexts. Reinforcement learning agents handle physical control and optimization. Language model agents handle knowledge work and communication. Both matter to the future of AI.
