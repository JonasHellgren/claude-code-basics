# Found Books

## 1. Reinforcement Learning Explained: A Practical Problem-Solving Approach

- **Author:** Jonas Hellgren, Johannes Lindgren
- **Year:** 2026 (published June 28, 2026)
- **Publisher:** CRC Press (Taylor & Francis / Routledge)
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Description:** A practical, problem-solving introduction to reinforcement learning explicitly designed for readers without a coding or university-level mathematics background. It explains how RL agents learn optimal behavior through interaction with an environment and reward feedback, and covers real-world application areas such as robotics, energy systems, finance, and autonomous vehicles, using many small, focused examples that build intuition and practical skill step by step.
- **Source:** https://www.routledge.com/Reinforcement-Learning-Explained-A-Practical-Problem-Solving-Approach/Hellgren-Lindgren/p/book/9781032996653

## 2. An Introduction to Deep Reinforcement Learning

- **Author:** Vinod K. Mishra
- **Year:** 2025 (published December 17, 2025)
- **Publisher:** Chapman & Hall/CRC (Taylor & Francis)
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Description:** An introductory guide to deep reinforcement learning (DRL) intended as a starting point for undergraduate and early graduate students who are new to the field. It surveys machine learning basics before walking through the mathematical and algorithmic foundations of DRL, single-agent and multi-agent algorithms, recent developments, and real-world applications.
- **Source:** https://www.routledge.com/An-Introduction-to-Deep-Reinforcement-Learning/Mishra/p/book/9781032651439

## 3. Mathematical Foundations of Reinforcement Learning

- **Author:** Shiyu Zhao
- **Year:** 2025 (published January 21, 2025)
- **Publisher:** Springer Nature Singapore (jointly with Tsinghua University Press)
- **Level:** Beginner
- **Description:** A friendly, mathematically grounded introduction to reinforcement learning that requires no prior RL background. It is organized into two parts: foundational concepts (Bellman equation, Bellman optimality equation, stochastic approximation) and classic algorithms (value/policy iteration, Monte Carlo methods, temporal-difference learning, value function approximation, policy gradient, and actor-critic methods), with a recurring grid-world example used throughout to keep the material approachable.
- **Source:** https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-981-97-3944-8
